## Problem

A full closet and nothing to wear. Every morning people solve the same puzzle again: what works for the weather, the nine o'clock meeting and dinner later. So they wear a few safe outfits, the rest just hangs there — and a month later they buy one more similar thing.

Wardrobe apps promise to fix this, but nearly all of them stumble at the same spot: to get any value you first have to enter your clothes by hand. It's slow and boring, and people quit before they ever see a first outfit.

## Task

Design a mobile app that:

- builds a daily outfit from clothes you already own, taking weather and plans into account;
- delivers value before you get tired of filling in your wardrobe;
- feels like a fashion magazine, not a spreadsheet of clothes.

A constraint I set myself: onboarding in four steps at most and under a minute, no long questionnaires.

## Onboarding

Instead of a questionnaire — four short questions, one per screen. Your occupation hints at the weekday dress code, “what do you wear” filters out the rest, and three favourite brands set the style and price range. That's enough for a first outfit to appear right away, not after uploading a hundred photos.

The questions are conversational, and the key word in each is set in italics so the screen reads in a second. The button never hides: on the brands step it stays inactive and says exactly how many more to pick.

## Visual language

Black, white and grey — no accent colour: in this app colour belongs to the clothes. A condensed grotesque in the logo, Inter for the interface and a serif italic for the meaningful words give it an editorial voice. No rounded corners: square buttons, fields and tiles make the interface stricter and closer to a magazine layout than to a “friendly” app.
